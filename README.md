# Fandom and Participatory Culture — Glossary

A bilingual study reference for **CULS5227 Fandom and Participatory Culture**,
MA in Intercultural Studies, the Chinese University of Hong Kong — 220 terms
across nine lessons, in English and 中文, in teaching order.

**→ Open the glossary:** http://mtlibao.github.io/culs5227-glossary

---

## Who this is for

This is course material for **students enrolled in CULS5227**. It follows one
syllabus and one reading list: the sections run in the order the course is
taught, definitions assume that week's reading, and citations point back to the
texts assigned for that week. Read away from the course, much of it will not
mean a great deal.

## How to use it

**Browse by lesson.** The course map on the left lists the ten lessons in
teaching order, L01 to L10. Click one to jump to it; the section you are
reading is highlighted as you scroll. L03 and L11 are the two Discussion Forums
and carry no terms of their own — that is deliberate, not a gap, and each
section says so.

**Or search everything at once.** The search box filters all 220 entries as you
type and matches English terms, 中文 terms, and the text of the definitions —
so searching `勞動` or `labour` finds every entry that discusses it, not only
the ones with that word in the title. Matches are highlighted.

**Follow the links between terms.** Underlined words inside a definition jump
to that term's own entry, and most entries end with a line of related concepts
— often in a different week, because the course keeps returning to the same
ideas from new angles.

**Check where a term comes from.** Where an entry carries a **Where the term
comes from** section, it names the scholar who coined, set out, or described
the concept — with their dates, the work it comes from, and, where relevant,
who carried it into this week's reading. Every one of these has been checked
against a source published after this glossary's own knowledge cutoff, not
recalled from memory — several scholars turned out to have died more recently
than expected, and those dates are corrected here. Where no single scholar can
be credited, the entry instead lists **Argues with**: the other names and
schools the reading itself is in dialogue with for that term.

**Check the locus.** Most definitions carry the page or paragraph they come
from — `(p. 33)` for print, `[1.1]` for the *Transformative Works and Cultures*
articles, which number paragraphs instead of pages. Quotation marks mean the
words are the author's own.

## Offline

One HTML file, no internet needed after you have it. Save it and open it in any
browser; it works on a phone as well as a laptop, and follows your light or
dark setting.

## Corrections

If an entry misreads a text, tell Dr Li. The glossary is rebuilt from the
course readings each time, so a correction sticks.
